# The Winged Races & the Winged Wars

> *"And the sky was blue. And the zephyrs true. And the air was clear. And the enemy near."*
> — a snippet of Aarakocran oral tradition

## People of the Feather

Long ago in the Age of Eden, Aarakocra were born of elevated birds by the first Gods of the Skies, to dwell and spiral in the zephyrs and clouds where Elemental Air would skim the surface of the world and do their bidding.

Harpies, however, are the result of those First Men that meddled with the powers of flight: through pilgramage to the cloudscapes of the outer plane of Celestia, by blessing of the Gods that rivalled the makers of the Aarakocra, by becaming stewards of the cloud giants, or even by connecting to or communing with the powers of Elemental Air themselves. Their souls, capturing the spirit of the bird as part of their transformation, learned to sing, a property the Aarakocra long felt should have been theirs, but it was not in their design, for they were eagles and raptors never did sing.

Those that were made from creatures of flight, born to fly, vs those who had acquired, stolen or were later bestowed the gift of flight made for natural rivals and enemies, not to mention the jealousy the true birds felt for the imposter’s singing voices. In the world before the Shattering, egged on by various factions and beings of power (including capricious dragons and wounded titans) the winged races went to war to learn who it would be that would dominate the skies.

## War in the Sky
This rivalry writ war was adjudicated by the Aarakocran pantheon, their maker and his allies, and the other Gods, and the two races were bound by honour and magic to fight only with wing and claw, and only in the skies.

After generations of aerial war, and rains of corpses and bloodied feathers, the Aarakocra won out. They  slew their foes, and in the anger and hate that had built up in them since they were chicks, groomed to go to war to avenge their parents, and then their chicks, and then theirs, they sought to wipe the Harpies out from the surface of the material world for the sin of trying to match them in their theatre, and for the bloody butchers bill that had grown.

The harpies fought, flew or died, and many turned bitter with shame and at the depths they sank to, including roosting on the ground, in order to survive. But the Aarakocra would not stop, and they brought slaughter out of the sky, and even against innocents that had forsaken the war.

With this the contract was broken. Many Gods in their fury and outrage intervened, but by then the Harpies of the material plane were all but gone.

## Final Flight of the Harpy
Some fled to the Shadowfell, and underwent the same pacts that kept those that dwelt there alive, becoming Raven Harpies, singers of memories long forgotten. Many fled to the Faewyld, where they found home in the Symbolic Courts as messengers and creatures of avarice and pride. And a few yet remained in the material, in their hiding places, and sang no more songs except those of powerful nearby creatures, to ward away enemies, and rarely dared the skies.

Of the Aarakocra, those that defied the Gods and shed so much innocent blood were struck down with divine punishment, cursed to become the Kenku, stripped of wing, will and the voice they never appreciated. Those few Aarakocra that had forsaken the war fled to remain distinct from their cousins whose wings were wilting and who were falling from the sky, and through luck and providence, were spared.

## Bitter Ends
The prideful bitterness that started, fuelled and finished that war reflects still in the peoples that perpetrated it. The Harpies are often mean spirited and bitter. The mute and flightless Kenku, a race now known for thievery and skulduggery, far outnumber the remaining Aarakocra who to this day follow lives and practice cultures of asceticism and pacifism, wary still in the depths of their souls of divine punishment and the bloody toll of pride. Even if the war is so long ago it is mere myth.

Few Harpies remain that are not embittered or twisted. For many, spite and glee in death is all they have left to cling to, and live pitiful mean lives. But there are those Harpies that rise above their racial ennui, which some consider a curse in of itself, and become adventurers. Such Harpies perhaps seek vengeance for crimes unremembered; perhaps they seek counsel with the Gods and Titans that courted them so long ago; and some, perhaps, innocent of the stain of embitterment, simply wish to see the world, and adventure for its own sake.

Of the Aarakocra, those that embraced aesceticism and pacifism lay the seeds of distant cultures that have in time grown once again proud, in distant cliff-side cities and aerial kingdoms. Such adventurers may yet be those that hede the gut feeling in the soul of the threat of divine punishment. Others may once again wish to bring the pride of the skies in front of the masses.

The kenku, of why they adventure little can be said, for little can they say. Many a kenku 'adventurer' finds themselves along for the ride, and a few become capable individuals able to work around their curse, talking and flying once again through alternative means. And of the powerful divine curse on their people, it has yet to shift after 1000s of years.

## The Harpy Bloodlines

The Three lines of Harpy that survive reflect the three paths their ancestors fled to, and the *song* they carry:

- **Raven Harpies** fled into the Shadow Realm. The Raven Queen sheltered their nests and gave them a duty they took up gratefully: to sing of the deeds and exploits, crimes and sins, of all who have died and passed through her realm. Their song drags long-forgotten memories thundering back into a listener's mind, primed for judgement and reflection in the Palace of Memories.
- **Dream Heralds** fled to the Fey Wild, where bitterness curdled into avarice. They found their place as messengers and bearers of news, and learned some of the fey's tricks. Their song is the song of the sublime, charming listeners into treating the singer as a dear friend and one who *must* be listened to.
- **Lost Harpies** survived in the material world, and then survived the Shattering, by keeping hidden and convincing all nearby that no Harpies lived there. They gave up song entirely, for it drew the ire of the Aarakocra, and turned their voices to mimicry - calling the curious away or immitating great and terrible predators.
