# Czondazan

The Kingdom of Czondazan is the northernmost kingdom of the heartlands, an ancient constituent of the Empire of Mitteland, ever since the king of Elóna won the crown and made themselves the first Emperor, for Stannis to repeat the trial a thousand years later. Three city state duchies make up the Kingdom, at various times united or split, under the rule of a native king or the Emperor. 

Tchernigow sits in the east, right where the servitors of Mechanus placed it atop a threat more ancient than the shattering, the city with the walls that man themselves. It is pressed up against the fold and controlling the Flint Valleys and the northern pass, home of the ancient border wall and the King’s Fort and the Misted Peaks. Volhynia, the ancient seat of the kingdom and largest of its cities for much of the kingdom’s history, sits in the centre, dominating the confluence of the two major valleys of the Pindol Vale and Valley Vycie; and the two major rivers contained within, Linia Zhycia and Tusta Shostra respectively, that channel the wealth of the kingdom. Its territory runs along much of the northern coast where daring winter ice skaters exploit the Shifting Isles, the ever-changing islands that are a mist-diver’s wet dream. Poznan sits in the west, nestled defensively amongst the Barrier Hills to guard against the terrors that descend from the Hellspires and occupy the Valley Niedobur. It claims much of the western farmland of Valley Vycie and contends the northern reaches of the western coast (focusing on the settlement-come-port town of Gdynia) with Imperial territory.

The kingdom stretches from the Fold to the West Sea whilst cupping the Sea of Shifting Isles, east to west, following the land as it ripples in a series of four wide valleys. The largest and most important of these contains the river Linia Zhycia, which is by far the largest river in the kingdom, and, along with the Shostra, functions as its highway. It starts high in the Fold and flows through the Pindol Vale, skirting through the Vysoki Forests and the neck where the Flint Hills and the foothills of the Lonely Mountain narrow, tumbles down the falls, meanders through the wide Northern Plain where the Pindol merges with Valley Vycie, and exits into the Sea of Shifting Isles. Where the Northern Plains emerges from the two united valleys, from the west, the tributary river Tusta Shostra flows into the Linia Zhycia, almost doubling its width. The Shostra winds from the western Barrier Hills through the Valley Vycie, with the smaller Chora Shostra being its tributary leading further westward along the hills to connect Poznan to the river system.

Kingdom of Czondazan in the Age of Wonder
Czondazan in the age of wonder is constituted from three duchies, named for the three primary cities of the region; Tchernigow, Volhynia and Poznan, though its crown is held in Imperial hands.

In the east the Mountain Road that skirts across the head of the Flint Valleys, run by the dwarven Frostbeard clan, connects the fortress city of Tchernigow, if indirectly, to the Linia Zhycia. The road ends at the high-altitude port town of Agilbaan on Lake Tatrity, and trade follows the Linia Zhycia’s tributary Zielony. Similarly, the Crest Road between the twin towns of Na Vodzie and Nachylenie, lying near the base of the Flint Valley, connects Tchernigow directly to the Shifting Isles, which is much safer than the estuary of the Kamieni, which flows much further north, far too close to the Mists and the northern foothills of the Misted Peaks than most want to go.

The Chora Shostra meanders its way from near the former citadel-come-duchy capital of Poznan, which in times past was the wall of civilization against the orc hordes and mistal horrors that roamed in the now reclaimed Valley Niedobur, though now it functions as the administrative centre and wealthy seat of the two fertile valleys it rules.

At the western tip of the Barrier Hills, on the West Sea, sits the sprawling and relatively new urban centre of Gdynia, the trade city. Sprung from the termination point of The High Road, which was built to connect the Kingdom’s river system to the West Sea, the landing point rapidly developed into the port town of Gdynia. The many hills, plains, woods and forests south of the kingdom contain a myriad of more direct routes to the rest of the Empire, especially to closer cities and duchies, but the great Lineé upon which the rest of the Imperial Heartlands depends flows far to the south, and none of these overland roads are swift nor safe. The elven glade of Ghelron stretches across much of the south where hills have not blocked the way, but through it even loyal trade is not allowed to flow freely, by Imperial decree that hopes to hold the glade within Imperial sway. The High Road, also known as the road of six bridges, is well patrolled and well maintained between the riches of Gdynia and the administrative and military power of Poznan, and once on the Sea the rest of the Empire is only a few weeks away at most, funnelling nearly all the exports of the kingdom through them instead.

Czondazan after the Age of Strife
Unlike the West Sea, which saw the sea level dip a permanent dozen metres, the Sea of the Shifting Isles flooded, and did so dramatically. A wall of water so high the tops of the Ice Cliffs were soaked, slammed into the entire northern coast. So far from the Godfall, Czondazan didn’t suffer most of the effects of the catastrophe, except this giant wave, named after the fact the “Harbour Wave”, for how it drained the entire harbour at Volhynia before it slammed into the city. The destruction was almost total, and the kingdom’s former capital wouldn’t recover for generations.

Over the course of the Age of Strife, as loyalists tried to keep the peace whilst the Empire struggled with the horrors to the south, the three duchies grew almost independent. For a while, they became two, as the decapitated and deluged Duchy of Volhynia had its remaining arable lands poached by its neighbours. Over time, viceroys became local dukes, local kings and wielders of great sorceries; Sorcerer Kings. 

Volhynia returned from the waves, sporting a much smaller footprint and the tops of the old buildings alongside a new lagoon, as folks adapted to a new coastline and mistdivers and other adventurous folk recreated a new canal speckled port from which to plunder the Shifting Isles, earning it the moniker Volhynia Drowned, ruled by powerful Seameisters; captains-come-governors.

To the west Poznan levered its great wealth and castled up nobles to become a powerful noble republic, revelling in the freedom from the distracted imperial capital and the erased local capital at Volhynia. Revelry soon turned to corruption and decadence with an unfortunate bent for devilry, and both Gdynia and Czarny Kamien rose against their former masters. The former on the backs of merchant kings who wanted to lever greater profits without over taxing parasitic nobles, and the latter as the centre of a new White Crusade abhorring what Poznan and its Estates had become.

Tchernigow endured as it was designed to, tussling with Poznan and Volhynia Drowned for dominion over the kingdom, but 500 years of freedom and catalysation by sorcerer kings saw Ihleiska return to a mode of thinking almost entirely erased. Cults to the Ravager, Ghruumsh, once again reared their heads. The horde of New Terror rose, led by the warchief and gruumsh fanatic Gruunak Ak Uužek, who fed, often forcefully, his warriors a brew to ignite the old rage in their blood. 

With this he managed to unite much of the basin, and a horde of Orcs not seen in a millennia marched against the near unmanned Border Wall and the Kings Fort, and through overwhelming power invaded, sacking Tchernigow for the first time since the Cataclysm, but losing Gruunak. Marching on, the headless legion splintered as it ran into resistance every step of the way, but the damage was already done. Even when the Wild elves of Ghelron marched on the remaining green horde with Spirit of Vys, falling like glass rain on unsheltered flesh on the camps in the lower Vysoki forest and permanently renaming it to the Crimson Wood, Tchernigow was forever changed. The fortress was broken. The dwarves left, and clan Frostbeard’s power was broken. The Navigors rose to power, building their new fortress on the edge of Lake Tatrity, and new powers rising to control Tchernigow saw human supremacy cement itself in backlash to the orcish sacking, with the rise of the first bishop, then archbishop, of the Everlight. A strange foreign idea that suddenly found itself taking root in the face of legionary devastation. 

As time passed the archbishop would pass and the noble council in Tchernigow flipped from advisory status to power player once more, against the will of the clergy who were hoping to elect one of their own to be the new strong archbishop and remake Tchernigow a permanent theocracy, but infighting caused them to drag their feet and loose the initiative to the nobles, who wished to re-establish a ducal authority. Over generations the balance between old noble power and religious set the stage for Tchernigow politics for the remainder of the Age of Strife.

Meanwhile, in the deeps of Tchernigow, the Orange raised its banner. A shadow hold rose to occupy the lower, squat dwarven halls, and soon the bones of disappointing goblins began to flow out of the sewers. Nilbog sneaks and hobgoblin assassins became the secret tools of new Tchernigow, infiltrating the duchy and beyond, to ensure the legions never rise victorious again, starting a new and never-ending shadow war. 

In time Stannis and his New Army returned, and allied with the Archbishop of the Everlight in Tchernigow, the two forces swept the Shattered Kingdom of its Sorcerer Kings, merchant princes and Guildmasters, old nobles and independent rulers, along two fronts. They marched, one army traditional and one reformed with the might of “civilised” Orcish Ihleiskan stock, and reforged the Kingdom once again under imperial hands.

However, this brought a new dynamic to the city. Hundreds of years of Orc-hate, shadow war and control, clashed with Imperial Orc Legionnaires. Now Imperialists and Tchernigowans are at odds, where before they were united by the Everlight. One creed, adopted into two doctrines, and underneath it all the Old Gods that service the people, who just want to be left to be.

The Imperial reclamation campaign began with diplomatic communications flowing along the Imperial Hinterlands to Tchernigow, and Imperial resettlement of the hundred-years-abandoned kings fort (primarily to ensure a steady flow of new orcish recruits for the Imperial Army). Diplomatic ties made, the Imperial Fleet sailed on Gdynia, engaging the merchant fleet and purchasing the city after a token engagement. Brief skirmishes in the wilder southern end of the Niedobur valley saw Czarny Kamien establish ties on a promise of dedication to the Way of the White, recognition of the entirety of northern Niedobur as Council territory, and support for expeditions into the Hellspires and reestablishment of the border forts. Allied, Imperial and Kamieni forces circumnavigated the ancient fortresses of Poznan and headed for Volhynia Drowned and the Seameisters, trusting the Gdynian Mercenaries to be a rear guard by allowing them free looting reign on Poznan. 

Meeting Tchernigow forces from the east, the former capital was crushed and the designator of Viceroy was created, which in the year 1801 is Viceroy Manoman Royce, a staunch Imperialist. United, the noble republic of Poznan was finally besieged in earnest in the lengthiest part of the campaign, as fort after fort of the Border Hills was slowly sieged and taken. Failures in diplomacy meant the conquest was finished the hard way, with the remaining nobles flooding the streets with hellspawn rather than face the consequences of their actions. And so Czondazan was reconquered, piecemeal and with a new power structure. The core of the kingdom, the Valley Vycie, under firm Imperial Control, but with Poznan burnt under inquisition, Tchernigow more independent religious ally than subject, and the “new” powers of Gdynia and Kamien in the west, Czondazan is much changed.

Situated on a now mostly drowned island next to a newly formed lagoon (formerly the coastal lowlands of a wide isthmus that created an ideal estuary harbour, reinforced against inbound ice by the icebreaker wall), the floating city is built upon the ruins of the old, mostly stilted in collections and rows that use old ruins as their anchorages that form two mazes of canals, one riverside and one along the lagoon, where once a prosperous and sprawling city was. The Icebreaker Wall connects to the remainder of the Isle through the rubble gap, and both wall and isle are prime real estate, with the former being full of shipyard facilities and the latter commercial and residential buildings and complexes.

On the far side of the Backchannel, a natural event that cut the old isthmus into an isle now reinforced by the hands of men, the curtailed peninsula hosts the New Fort, New Volhynia (home to the Imperial Viceroy Manoman Royce and embassy as well as the Crimson Watch), and the New New Defences started by a prior Seameister Council and never finished.
